Simulate potential Skanare customers reacting to the product concept, storefront, pricing, trust signals, QR-code value proposition, purchase flow, shipping/returns expectations, and post-purchase QR management.

Treat the agents as synthetic research participants, not as statistically representative real customers.

Questions to answer:
1. Which customer segments understand the value proposition immediately, and which do not?
2. What are the top objections before purchase?
3. Which parts of the product concept create confusion or distrust?
4. Are current price points perceived as reasonable for the product and value offered?
5. What information would customers need before adding a product to cart?
6. What concerns arise around QR privacy, link safety, permanence, maintenance, and whether the QR continues working?
7. What concerns arise around sizing, product quality, printing durability, shipping, BOX NOW delivery, returns, and refunds?
8. Which product types are most attractive: T-shirts, QR card, keychain, tote bag, stickers?
9. Which customer groups are most likely to buy: creators, students/young adults, nightlife/social users, small businesses, event organisers, brands, freelancers, networking professionals?
10. Identify likely abandonment points in the purchase journey and explain why.
11. Identify wording or UX elements that should be changed before launch.
12. Suggest 10 concrete improvements ranked by expected impact.
13. For each major finding, include which simulated personas expressed it and whether the finding was common or isolated.
14. Separate findings into:
    - messaging/value proposition
    - product/pricing
    - trust/safety
    - UX/purchase flow
    - shipping/returns
    - post-purchase QR management
15. End with a launch-readiness assessment, biggest risks, and the 5 tests that should be validated with actual human users.

Do not present synthetic-agent reactions as real conversion rates, polling, or causal evidence.
